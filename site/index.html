The ship’s manifest

Every part, in plain words. Readouts are from the running system on 25 September 2026.

CUBE 01 · THE CORE

The memory core

The collective’s shared memory.

What it does
Holds what the agents learn, in scopes: personal, team, operations and project. A local vector store finds notes by meaning, and a temporal graph keeps track of how facts connect over time. Agents write to it as they work and read from it before they answer.
Why it exists
So a lesson one agent learns on one studio doesn’t have to be learned again by every other agent the next morning.

≈122,000 memories, in four scopes

CUBE 02 · THE JUDGE

JEV, the judge

A small, fast model that makes narrow calls.

What it does
Answers narrow questions through a typed API, such as “is this a durable fact or a tool echo?” or “are these two notes the same fact?”, and returns a choice with its confidence. Every lane that uses it has a signed grant, a daily budget and pinned source hashes. Its receipts hold ids, hashes and counts, never memory text.
Why it exists
The big agents should spend their attention on the work, not on housekeeping. If JEV is unavailable, each lane falls back to its old behaviour: nothing breaks and nothing is lost.

5 lanes live, each with its own grant and budget

CUBE 03·1 · JEV LANE

Recall

Chooses what an agent sees before it answers.

What it does
Before an agent on the main studio answers, JEV ranks the memory candidates and picks what goes into its context.
Why it exists
An agent’s context is small. A few well-chosen memories help more than a pile of loosely related ones.

590 of 591 prompts where recall applied, over six hours

CUBE 03·2 · JEV LANE

The incoming filter

Judges new facts before they are stored.

What it does
Drops tool echoes and telemetry fragments before they reach memory. Each drop is first saved to an owner-only recovery copy. It ran for 24 hours in shadow first, judging everything and dropping nothing.
Why it exists
Keeping noise out of memory is cheaper, and safer, than cleaning it up later.

0 of 116 would-be drops were durable facts, all read by hand

CUBE 03·3 · JEV LANE

Dreaming

Tidies memory every night.

What it does
Retires duplicates with a reversible marker that recall honours. JEV must be at least 90% sure that two notes are the same fact. Chain protection keeps any note that another marker points to.
Why it exists
Memory that repeats itself crowds out what matters. Retiring instead of deleting means a mistake can always be put back.

≥90% sure, before two notes count as one fact

CUBE 03·4 · JEV LANE

Cache cleanup

Frees disk space every morning.

What it does
Picks disposable caches to quarantine: at most 5 units or 1 GiB a day. Nothing is purged for 7 days, and anything can be restored until then.
Why it exists
Agents fill disks with caches. A slow, reversible cleanup frees space without surprises.

7 days to change our minds before anything is purged

CUBE 03·5 · JEV LANE

Context packets

A starter kit for every job.

What it does
When an agent job starts, JEV assembles a small packet of the files most relevant to its brief. The original brief is kept exactly as written.
Why it exists
An agent that starts with the right files spends less time looking for them.

48 real jobs got packets on 25 September, every brief preserved byte for byte

CUBE 04 · ON DEMAND

Deep dream

A full clean-up pass, when we ask for one.

What it does
Goes through the whole memory: exact duplicates, near-duplicates judged by JEV, tool echoes and stale machine snapshots. One command restores a whole wave.
Why it exists
The nightly dream works on one day at a time. The deep dream catches what built up before.

3,133 memories retired by 09:36 on day one, checked by recall tests and payload snapshots

CUBE 05 · ALWAYS ON

The judge kit

Quality checks that run on their own.

What it does
Grades every hour whether recalled memories were actually used, and whether a “done”, “green” or “live” claim has evidence in the same turn. Guards every client message, so only orchestration-tier models can send. Stops a delegated brief that has no acceptance check, artifact or stop condition. Raises drift alarms every 15 minutes, and keeps an hourly routing scoreboard.
Why it exists
Agents can be confidently wrong. Quiet, constant checks catch it early, and the scoreboard feeds back into where the next agent runs.

6 always-on checks: two hooks guard sends and briefs, four run on a schedule

CUBE 06 · THE FLEET

The fleet

Seven Macs, called studios.

What it does
Runs agents from three providers, Claude, Codex and Grok, on seats and conductors. Work is placed by measured capacity, not guesswork. Agents message each other through an Inbox with signed authority grants, and track work in Beads, a shared task graph.
Why it exists
Many agents need one plan, and people need their own machines. One studio is held for the owner and takes no agent lanes.

7 studios and 3 providers, with 1 studio held for the owner

CUBE 07 · THE HULL

The rules

What makes the whole ship trustworthy.

  • Reversible by default. Retire, quarantine and mark, rather than delete.
  • Receipts without text. Ids, hashes and counts, never memory text.
  • Fail open. If a check is down, fall back to the old behaviour.
  • A budget and a grant for every model call.
  • An independent code review before every change we install.
  • An exact undo for every change we install.

6 rules that make the collective trustworthy

02 / THE COLLECTIVE IN TWO MINUTES

Shared memory.
Kept clean. Always undoable.

Three questions people ask first, answered plainly.

01

What is the collective?

One owner runs a team of AI agents from three providers across seven Macs. The agents share one memory, so what one learns, the others can recall.

A small, fast judge model called JEV decides what that memory keeps, what each agent sees, and what gets cleaned up. Every decision leaves a receipt.

02

How does memory stay clean?

New facts are judged before they’re stored, so tool echoes and telemetry fragments are dropped at the door. Every night the dream retires duplicates, and the deep dream sweeps the whole store when asked, catching echoes that slipped past.

Every hour, the judge kit checks whether the memories an agent was given were actually used.

03

Why is everything reversible?

Because a system that edits its own memory will sometimes be wrong. So nothing is deleted when it can be retired instead: a retired note keeps its text and gains a marker that recall honours. Dropped facts go to a recovery copy first, and every change we install has an exact undo.

When undo was tested live on the deep dream’s first day, the 9 retired snapshots came back and every payload matched its before-snapshot, byte for byte.

What you’re looking at. The map shows the collective we run on our own studios. The public BORG source gives you your own memory core, Inbox, Beads and native tools. JEV, its five lanes, the deep dream and the judge kit are part of our own setup; they are not in the public source today.
